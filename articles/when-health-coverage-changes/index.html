What to do when your health coverage changes

A new card can change which clinicians are in network, how much help you receive, and how quickly you are supposed to tell the state. The card does not, by itself, tell you whether this clinic can bill the plan.

Canby Community Clinic · Health Education

Health coverage usually changes on a deadline, not on a convenient afternoon. Someone loses a job, Medi-Cal stops, a household grows, or an address changes, and the plan that paid for last month’s visit may not pay for the next one. Covered California’s account guidance tells enrollees to report those changes, and it explains why the report matters: a move can change which plans are offered, income can change financial help, and the same facts can move a person between Covered California and Medi-Cal.

This article is about those public rules, and about the narrower question of a visit at Canby Community Clinic, 7601 Canby Ave #6B in Reseda. The clinic’s insurance page says to bring the card, photo identification, and a medicine list, and to call to check the plan and ask about costs. Accepted plans are not listed on this website. Nothing below adds a plan to that list.

Tell the right office, on its clock

Covered California’s account guide splits the clock by program. People enrolled through Covered California are told to sign in and update the account within 30 days of the change. People with Medi-Cal are told to notify the county within 10 days. County offices are listed by the California Department of Health Care Services. The guide assigns those two updates to different places, so use the instruction that matches the program you have.

The same guide lists the kinds of changes to report: marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, gaining or losing a dependent, any income change including starting or losing a job, a new address, health insurance through a job, Medicare or Medi-Cal, disability status, tax-filing status, citizenship or immigration status, incarceration or release, American Indian or Alaska Native tribal status, and corrections to a name, date of birth, or Social Security number.

There is a financial reason to be prompt, not only a paperwork reason. Covered California says that if you receive more financial help than you qualify for, you may have to pay some or all of it back when you file federal taxes. Reporting a higher income late does not freeze the earlier months. The phone number published for account help is (800) 300-1506. This clinic does not operate that line and cannot see your Covered California account.

Some changes open a limited time to enroll or switch

Outside the regular open enrollment period, Covered California uses a special enrollment period tied to a qualifying life event. Its major-life-changes page lists common ones: losing job coverage, losing Medi-Cal, losing student coverage, COBRA ending, aging off a parent’s plan, marriage or a domestic partnership, divorce or legal separation, a birth, adoption, or foster placement, a death in the household that affects coverage, a move to California or a move within California that changes plan options, a new citizenship or lawful presence, loss of coverage tied to military service, release from incarceration, and certain losses of financial help. Federally recognized American Indian or Alaska Native membership is listed among the special situations. If that is your situation, use the official page rather than assuming a single deadline fits every household.

The page’s table describes several situations in which a person has about 60 days to act. It lists a longer window, within 90 days, when Medi-Cal ends, and it says Covered California may automatically select a plan in that situation. If you recently lost Medi-Cal, sign in and see which plan is there, and which clinicians it includes. Losing coverage and choosing to drop coverage are not described as the same event. The page lists “COBRA coverage ended” as a qualifying change and has its own explanation of what counts. Read that explanation before you assume a premium you stopped paying has opened a new enrollment.

Covered California also runs a regular open enrollment period, when plan changes are more widely available. This article does not print the dates. They are posted on coveredca.com and they are easy to get wrong from memory. California residents shop on Covered California, not on HealthCare.gov. HealthCare.gov is a useful glossary. It is not the application site for this state.

Plan type changes who you can see

HealthCare.gov’s description of marketplace plan types is the clearest short account of the usual network rules, and it is the source for the following sentences. An exclusive provider organization generally covers care only inside its network, with an exception for emergencies. A health maintenance organization usually limits coverage to clinicians who work for or contract with the plan, generally does not cover out-of-network care except in an emergency, and may require you to live or work in its service area. A point-of-service plan costs less inside the network and requires a referral from the primary care doctor in order to see a specialist. A preferred provider organization costs less inside the network, and you can go outside the network without a referral at an additional cost.

A new card can move you from one of those arrangements to another without feeling like a clinical event. The specialist who saw you in March may be outside the network in April. How a clinic referral works explains the referral piece. The network piece is simpler to state and harder to guess: call the number on the card and ask whether a particular clinic is in network, then call the clinic and ask whether that plan can be used there. Do both. An insurer’s directory and a clinic’s front desk can disagree, and the visit is the wrong moment to discover which one was current.

What to bring, and what this clinic will not put on a webpage

Bring the new card, photo identification, and your medication list to any visit you have already confirmed. If the visit is not confirmed, do not drive to Reseda on the theory that a website article created an appointment. Call (818) 674-4414 and ask about the plan by name. Ask what the visit may cost if the plan cannot be billed. The insurance page tells readers to have that conversation before the appointment. This article cannot quote a price, a copay, or a list of contracted plans, because the clinic has not published them here.

If you need help enrolling, Covered California’s account guide points to certified enrollers and to the same (800) 300-1506 line. The clinic’s community resources page links to Covered California enrollment and to the state’s Medi-Cal application page. Those are the application doors. A community clinic can explain where to click. It cannot approve Medi-Cal, and it should not be given your Social Security number through the contact form on this site.

If the coverage change is the reason you are looking for a clinician at all, start with what to bring to a first clinic visit once you know a visit is possible. The papers and the plan are related. They are not the same task.
